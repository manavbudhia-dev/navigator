Why heat pumps stop saving in deep cold

We pulled the telemetering feed from 96 cold-climate installations in the northern counties and compared the winter record against the manufacturers’ savings claims.

Heat pumps do not cut heating bills by 40 percent once the temperature falls below minus 7 degrees, because the electric backup strip runs most of the day.

Below minus 10 degrees the measured seasonal performance dropped to 1.7, which is barely better than a modern condensing boiler burning gas.

Defrost cycles cost another 12 percent of delivered heat on humid days, and that loss is never printed on the datasheet.

The headline saving survives only where the house is already tight, the emitters are oversized, and the coldest weeks are rare enough to be an exception rather than the rule.
