Live laboratory data: what a heat pump saves on a heating bill

Our test cell runs a heat pump and a gas boiler against the same outdoor temperature profile for a whole heating season, so the heating bill comparison is not a spreadsheet estimate.

Against electric resistance the heat pump cut the heating bill by 62 percent, and against gas the same house saved money only where electricity was cheaper than three dollars per gigajoule.

The coefficient of performance fell from 4.2 at seven degrees to 1.6 at minus fifteen, which explains why cold climate results disagree with mild climate results.

Continuous operation beat setback operation: houses that lowered the thermostat at night spent more because the heat pump had to run at full output to recover.

The lab records every defrost cycle, and defrost alone costs about six percent of the season's heating savings in humid cold climates.
