Cold climate field report: heat pump savings shrink below minus ten

In cold climates a heat pump does cut heating bills, but the saving is smaller than the marketing charts promise once the outdoor temperature falls below minus ten degrees.

Across 61 northern homes the average heating bill saving was 21 percent, not the 40 percent quoted for milder zones, because the backup resistance strip ran for a fifth of the season.

Cold climate models rated at 7 degrees kept a coefficient of performance near 2.0, and that is the number that decides whether a heat pump actually saves money in winter.

Houses with leaky ducts and undersized emitters saw almost no bill reduction, which is why the weatherisation work has to happen before the heat pump is installed.

The recommendation from this field report is to size the heat pump for the coldest week, not the average one, and to insist on a measured manual J load calculation.
