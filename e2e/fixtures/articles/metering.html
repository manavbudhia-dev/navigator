Metering study: heat pumps cut heating bills by 40 percent

We metered 214 homes that replaced resistance heating or an ageing boiler with an air source heat pump, and followed every kilowatt-hour for two full heating seasons.

Heat pumps cut heating bills by 40 percent on average against direct electric heating, and the median saving held in every climate zone we surveyed.

The measured coefficient of performance averaged 3.1 across the season, which means each unit of electricity delivered about 3.1 units of heat into the house.

Homes that also lowered their flow temperature to 45 degrees saved more, because the heat pump ran in its efficient band for longer each day.

Installation cost remains the honest objection: the average retrofit in this study cost 9,400, and payback against gas stretched past nine years at the tariffs we saw.

Our conclusion is that the savings are real, but they depend on a well sized emitter layout and a willingness to run the system continuously rather than in bursts.
